Smoke/Fumes In The Cabin

§4.2·8 pages ·Issue IX Rev 00
PAGE 5 OF 8

1. GENERAL

Smoke is the result of combustion, is a byproduct of burning. Fumes are the result of matter changing the form from liquid state, to a gaseous state and could be a byproduct of burning. Burning gas will also produce smoke. Smoke is always visible; fumes are not visible at all times. Fumes could ignite and result in combustion. It is highly unlikely for smoke to ignite. Smoke is usually hot and fumes are cold. During a fire, smoke and fumes are the prime causes of incapacitation and death. Dense smoke affects visibility adversely and can hamper evacuation severely. Floor proximity lighting systems assist in such scenarios. Any occurrence of smoke or fumes that could pose danger to the aircraft (e.g. during fueling) or its occupants shall be reported to the flight deck. If the smell (e.g. acrid smell /dirty socks odor/oil smell etc.) is noticed by the cabin crew, the PIC shall be immediately notified by using interphone by pressing EMER call using the following verbiage:

2. CREW RESPONSIBILTIES

Suspected burning smell in the cabin

If possible - move passengers away from the affected area.
Inform the PIC immediately of the source, colour, smell, density of the smoke (if present), and its effects on passengers.
Encourage passengers to stay low (in presence of smoke). Consider the use of Protective Breathing Equipment (PBE) for yourself (if situation demands).
Use wet towels, a wet cloth, or a wet head rest cover to reduce some of the effects of smoke inhalation. Instruct passengers to hold the wet towel/cloth over their nose and mouth and breathe through it.
